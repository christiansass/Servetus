# Biblical Research Wall and Apologetics: From the Way to First Century Fellowships

## Summary

Christian shared his Miro-based biblical research board with Ken Daniels live during this session, granting him editor access. The board is a long-running visual research project that began November 20, 2022, originally started when his kids asked him to do home fellowship. It functions as a living whiteboard — not a new presentation each session, but a single cumulative presentation that grows over time.

The board has a history of collaboration with other believers. Christian shared it with Anna and John Brown (founders of Compass Church in Kentucky) before they launched their church. He also mentioned Will Barlow as another figure connected to Compass. The board includes a "John Brown wing" where they were invited to add their own research. Christian noted that two of the people on the access list are now deceased — some of his closest friends.

The teaching approach Christian described is apologetics-based: he starts fellowship from the foundation up, beginning with a film called "Patterns of Evidence" to establish mosaic authorship as a credible starting point, then building out from there into how the Scriptures were formed. He noted that he learned this material entirely on his own through the internet — it was never taught to him formally, including not through The Way International, which he observed no longer offers seminary-level instruction now that the Way College of Emporia is gone.

Ken made the observation that what Christian is doing mirrors his own desire to find writers and researchers to work with — the Miro board is a collaborative infrastructure for exactly this kind of ongoing doctrinal and historical exploration.

Christian described the planned recording format: he will go through the board with Ken and Nick in structured sessions, building episode-by-episode from introduction through the full arc of the story — "from the Way to First Century Fellowships." He mentioned that episode 1 (with Nick) was an introduction, and he may redo it. The goal is that as people are brought up to speed, the recordings become a resource others can access.

He also raised a concern about naming: calling his channel the "Biblical Research Center" likely created friction with his uncle (implying a family connection to The Way International's legacy teaching channels), but he was clear that this is his heritage and his research — he is not pretending to represent something he is not.
