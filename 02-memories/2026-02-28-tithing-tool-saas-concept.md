## Related Research — Parable of the Talents and Historical Monetary Values

The session's second half developed a directly related thread: the real historical monetary value of a biblical talent, using live calculations during the call.

**Core teaching point:**
> "A talent is roughly the equivalent of 20 years of labor for the average worker." — Christian Sass, 21:14:25

**The calculation performed live (Feb 28, 2026 spot prices):**

| Metal | Spot Price (Feb 2026) | Talent in Denari | Calculated Value |
|-------|----------------------|-----------------|-----------------|
| Silver | ~$95/oz | 6,000 denari | ~$570,000 per talent |
| Gold | ~$5,286/oz | 6,000 denari | ~$31,716,000 per talent (initial estimate; overestimated) |

Nick Bates corrected the calculation after looking up denari weight via AI:
- One denari = 3.4–4.55 grams (not one ounce as assumed initially)
- Revised estimates: ~$72,000/talent in silver; ~$4,080,000/talent in gold

**Christian's interpretive framing:**
> "If it's 20 years of labor, what does that sound like to you? What would you call 20 years of labor in the modern vernacular? A lot — you would call it your career. Some people's career is measured in silver, and some people's career is measured in gold. The question is — symbolically, it represents the gifts or responsibility that God has entrusted [to an] individual." — Christian Sass, 21:24:20

**The parable application:**
Matthew 25:15 — God entrusts resources (careers, gifts, time) to individuals before the king returns. The servant who buried his talent is rebuked. Burying one's gifts — including citing uncertainty about theology — is not an acceptable excuse.

> "Could have given it to that Trinitarian over there, and he wouldn't have wasted it, right? What did you do with it? Well, I wasn't sure if I had the rightly divided Word of God yet." — Christian Sass, 21:26:07

**Nick's offer to contribute:**
