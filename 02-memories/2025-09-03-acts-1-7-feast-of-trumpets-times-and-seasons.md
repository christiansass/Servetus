# Acts 1:7 — Feast of Trumpets, Times and Seasons, Audience Context

**Source:** 2025-09-03-Acts_17_explanation.md
**Date:** 2025-09-03

## Summary

Exegetical discussion of Acts 1:7 in the context of the Feast of Trumpets as a Hebrew idiom for "no one knows the day or hour." Key insight: context (who Jesus is speaking to) matters enormously.

## The Verse

> *"And he said unto them, It is not for you to know the times or the seasons, which the Father hath put in his own power."* — Acts 1:7

**Context**: Acts 1:6 — disciples asked when the kingdom would be restored to Israel.

## Key Observations

### 1. Audience
- Jesus is speaking to Jewish disciples expecting national/political restoration of Israel
- "You" = His chosen witnesses, not generic humanity
- Their role: receive the Spirit → be witnesses (Acts 1:8), not calendar-watchers

### 2. Times and Seasons (Greek)
- **Chronos** (χρόνος) = measured time, chronology
- **Kairos** (καιρός) = appointed time, season, epoch
- Both measured timing AND appointed events belong to the Father's authority

### 3. The Redirect
> "Don't get stuck on the calendar — get on with the mission."

Jesus doesn't deny the restoration; He redirects their attention from timing to mission.

## Connection to Feast of Trumpets

- **Yom Teruah** (Feast of Trumpets) carries the idiom "no one knows the day or hour" because the new month began when the first sliver of the new moon was sighted — an inherently uncertain moment
- This idiom fits prophetic language about the Lord's return
- Growing acceptance of this Hebrew idiom framework among eschatologists
