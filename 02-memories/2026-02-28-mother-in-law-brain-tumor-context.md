# Wife in Quincy: Mother-in-Law Terminal Diagnosis and Marital Separation Context

## Summary

As of February 28, 2026, Christian's wife has been in Quincy, Illinois (their shared hometown) for approximately two weeks, caring for her mother who has received a terminal brain tumor diagnosis. Doctors gave a prognosis of six weeks or less. The mother-in-law had been in memory care for the prior year before this diagnosis.

Christian described the relationship with his mother-in-law as one defined by a deep character friction — she is someone for whom money has functioned as a primary driver, which he felt had damaged her relationships with her children and her husband (now deceased, who had a stroke in his final years). He was careful to distinguish between his initial framing and a more honest one: she is not someone for whom God is irrelevant, but someone for whom God does not displace fear about money.

The conversation reflected on the opportunity this situation presents for Christian's wife — to demonstrate an unconditional, God-sourced love in front of her sister and family members who do not share that foundation.

A secondary thread was Christian's own experience managing the household alone during this period. He described his wife as the person who maintains their family schedule ("a chain-fed Gatling gun schedule that she keeps me out of the loop of") and the disorientation of operating without her present. He used the experience of substitute-leading a robotics club session as an analogy: arriving without information and having to piece things together on his own — like being in an escape room.

He also reflected openly on the emotional and physical weight of extended separation in marriage, and how that experience clarified for him why Paul spoke the way he did about marriage — that once that bond exists, its absence is acutely felt.
