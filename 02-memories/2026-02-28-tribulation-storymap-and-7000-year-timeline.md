# Tribulation Period Storymap and 7000-Year Timeline Research

## Summary

Beginning around 20:16, the session shifts from technical Obsidian/Mural demonstration into the eschatological research driving the urgency behind the whole project. Christian shares the Miro storymap he has been building on the 7000-year biblical timeline and explains how it connects to the tribulation period scenario he is now "fully convinced" of.

This is the content layer that the Servetus system infrastructure is being built to serve.

## The Connecting Thread

> "I'm starting to get fully convinced of this tribulation period scenario, right? This is the key that connects this board and this board. I never intended to study the 7000-year week when I did this at all — that wasn't my point."

The timeline research began organically through his wife making notes in a green notebook, Christian charting them, and recognizing patterns — lifespans after the flood, the Tower of Babel, the Davidic kingdom, Hezekiah, the placement of Yeshua.

## The Miro Timeline Board

Christian is showing a hand-built Miro board charting biblical chronology from the flood through the present. Known landmarks:

- Post-flood lifespan changes (theory involving an "isolator" — a separate note exists)
- Tower of Babel
- Davidic kingdom (acknowledged as not yet accurately placed)
- Hezekiah (needs start and end dates)
- Yeshua — both parents with a line tracing back to David

> "This is the mother of all infographics here. Because what I'm trying to do is figure out, if I only have two years gap here, what am I going to do with it to prepare people going through this tribulation period?"

Outstanding data needs: start and end dates for each monarch/event to place them accurately on the timeline.

## The Two-Year Frame

The research is organized around a specific operational window: approximately two years before an anticipated Yom Teruah return (tentatively 2028). The motivation is not speculative — Christian treats it as functionally real regardless of certainty:

> "I'll be happy to be wrong. We live our lives that way."

The work being done now — the videos, the research, the knowledge base — is intended to serve as a resource for those who remain through the tribulation period:

> "All of these videos here — that becomes their research center in lieu of our absence."

And for what comes after:

> "1000 years with the Lord, dude... Me doing this is me stewarding the talents that he gave me."

## The Ministry Research Arm Tension

Ken introduces a live situation: a member of the clergy has circulated a draft essay challenging the Way International leadership about the deficiency of the ministry's research arm. The concern: they are regurgitating old scholarship rather than expanding and exploring new territory.

Ken's framing:

> "It's the old wine skin issue... We've got doctor's work, we've got all this. But it's like we've lost the mentality — lost the elasticity in the skin to take in new wine."

Christian's self-identification: "So I'm new wine in the ministry's old wine skins."

The proposed solution is not internal reform but lateral assembly: building the First Century Fellowship server as a gathering point for people already doing the work across ministry boundaries. Michael Cosgrove is named as a key person to bring in, and Shane Height is also mentioned.

> "First Century is the solution to these difficulties... Folks in the Way International who serve — they don't need to feel like, oh, whatever. It's like, no — it's coming for your lunch. Uniting. We're uniting. It's about unity."

## Ezekiel 38-39 Sidelight

Ken mentions that recent events are being considered as "stage setting for Ezekiel 38-39" (the Gog-Magog war scenario). Christian acknowledges it is coming and folds it into the two-year urgency frame.
