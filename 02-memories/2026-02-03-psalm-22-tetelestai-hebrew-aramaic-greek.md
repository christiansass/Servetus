# Psalm 22 and Tetelestai — Hebrew, Aramaic, Greek Linguistic Analysis

**Source:** 2026-02-03-Psalm_22_and_Tetelestai.md
**Date:** 2026-02-03

## Summary

Scholarly linguistic analysis of whether the Hebrew closing line of Psalm 22 matches Christ's "It is finished" (tetelestai, John 19:30), including Aramaic reconstruction. Conclusion: yes — same meaning, same tense, same completion motif across all three languages.

## Key Points

### Hebrew (Psalm 22:31)
- Final words: **כִּי עָשָׂה** (*ki asah*) — "for He has done it" / "because it has been accomplished"
- **עָשָׂה (asah)** = to do, accomplish, bring to completion
- Grammatical perfect tense — completed action
- Object intentionally unstated — open-ended, messianic

### Greek (John 19:30)
- **τετέλεσται (tetelestai)** — perfect tense, passive/reflexive
- Meaning: "It has been completed / accomplished / brought to its intended end"
- First-century usage: a debt fully paid, a task fully completed, a mission at its goal

### Aramaic (reconstructed)
- Jesus almost certainly spoke Galilean Aramaic on the cross
- Most likely roots: **ܫܠܡ (shlem)** (complete, bring to wholeness) or **ܓܡܪ (gamar)** (finish, complete)
- No preserved Aramaic quotation exists — only Greek record (John)
- Every viable Aramaic reconstruction lands on the same semantic ground as Psalm 22:31

### The Theological Connection
- Psalm 22 **begins**: "My God, my God, why have you forsaken me?" — quoted aloud by Christ
- Psalm 22 **ends**: "He has done it." — declared by John in Greek as tetelestai
- In Jewish interpretive culture: quoting the **first line** of a psalm invokes the **whole psalm**, especially its conclusion
- Christ quoting the opening was a deliberate bookending — pointing His audience to the entire arc of the psalm as fulfilled

### Bottom Line
| Language | Text | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Hebrew | כִּי עָשָׂה (ki asah) | He has done it / It is accomplished |
| Aramaic (reconstructed) | shlem / gamar equivalent | It is completed |
| Greek | τετέλεσται (tetelestai) | It is finished |

Same meaning. Same tense. Same theological weight. Same completion motif.
