# Tech Tariffs and Sovereignty — Real Arguments vs. Anti-American Framing

**Source:** 2026-01-29-Tech_Tariffs_and_America.md
**Date:** 2026-01-29

## Summary

Discussion about why foreign actors and critics call for "getting off US tech," whether that's genuinely anti-American or reflects real structural concerns, and how an American builder of alternatives fits into that picture.

## Key Points

### The "Against US Tech" label is lazy but the concern is real

Most European/international critics aren't against American *innovation* — they're against:
- US-based hyperscalers
- Jurisdictional control (CLOUD Act, FISA 702, National Security Letters)
- Corporate consolidation of identity, storage, search, communication, payments, AI inference
- State–corporate entanglement

### The strongest legitimate arguments

**A. Jurisdiction and extraterritoriality (the big one)**
- CLOUD Act: US companies can be compelled to produce data regardless of where it's stored
- FISA 702, National Security Letters: no public judicial review
- Foreign governments say: "We don't want another country's courts to have theoretical authority over our data"
- This is sovereignty logic — the same logic applied by American digital-first builders

**B. Systemic concentration risk (cuts across borders)**
- One handful of companies controls identity, storage, search, communication, payments, and AI
- From a foreign national's view: that's foreign critical infrastructure dependence, not free market innovation
- Same argument American builders make — just from a national rather than spiritual angle

### The American builder's position

- Building an American alternative means you *agree* with the sovereignty critique — applied to your own country
- The offense is justified: the framing collapses American builders with Big Tech hyperscalers
- The solution: distinguish between *US-based concentration* (the problem) and *American innovation* (the solution)
