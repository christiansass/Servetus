# Philippines Romance Scam — Due Diligence Framework for a Friend

**Source:** 2026-02-02-Verifying_Crowdfunding_Stories.md
**Date:** 2026-02-02

## Summary

Discernment session about a friend (Tim Phillips) who may be the victim of a long-con Philippines-based romance/proxy-family scam. A WayGiver crowdfunding campaign was circulating for Tim's alleged infant daughter Zia, who supposedly had hydrocephalus surgery and needs $6K more for an intestinal blockage procedure. The user identified Tim as a "mule" — not the scammer, but the carrier legitimizing the story to others. Session developed a non-confrontational verification strategy using a recorded video platform.

## Key Points

### Red Flags Identified
- Marriage occurred in person once; child allegedly born without Tim ever being physically present
- Tim has never met the child in person, never been present at hospital
- No FaceTime-with-hospital-context has occurred; no video showing baby + medical environment simultaneously
- Campaign (WayGiver) contains no hospital name, city, doctor names, or documentation
- Story structure matches textbook long-con romance/proxy-family scam escalation: bonding → commitment milestone → medical crisis → time pressure → moral pressure → spiritual pressure
- Philippines identified as significant context: organized long-con scam ecosystems active there

### Scam Mechanics Identified
- **FaceTime is ideal for long-cons**: no native recording, private emotional container, narrative drift is invisible, each call becomes "the new truth"
- **Mule dynamic**: Tim may not be lying — he's emotionally compromised and carrying the payload
- **Child-as-leverage**: strongest possible lever targeting faithful, provider-instinct, Christian-conscience men

### Verification Strategy (Non-Confrontational)
1. **Onboard Tim onto a recorded platform** — frame as infrastructure for calls and prayer updates, not investigation
2. **Normalize it as "how we do calls"** — no drama about recording
3. **Let Tim invite his wife** — never push for direct access to her; keep Tim as the bridge
4. **Observe friction** — delays, sudden crises, camera "not working," pre-recorded clips, refusal to show surroundings, anger when recording mentioned

### Key Insight
> A recorded, third-party-hosted call introduces accountability, real-time presence, and environmental consistency. That's kryptonite to fraud. Scams rely on platforms they control, asynchronous communication, and image drops instead of live interaction.

### Minimum Verification Requirements
- Live video call with baby, mother, hospital room, current date spoken aloud
- Hospital name and city (allows independent policy verification)
- Invoice photo with patient name and dates
- If any of these are refused with spiritual guilt, urgency deflection, or "the hospital won't allow it" — that is not how real hospitals behave
