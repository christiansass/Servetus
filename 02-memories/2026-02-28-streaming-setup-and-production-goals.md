# Streaming Setup and Production Goals — 1st Century Fellowship

The February 28 planning session opened with Nick Bates raising the need to improve his remote recording environment and the group acknowledging that all three founders need to look like credible streamers for future video production.

## Key Points

**Nick's setup needs:**
- Nick mentioned he has 3D-printable files for honeycomb-shaped lights he wants to hang in his recording space ("my dungeon").
- Camera upgrade is on his list — his current setup "works but I've got to do something better."
- Streaming infrastructure needs to be built out.

**The first video was a beta test:**
Christian noted that the first video he and Nick shot together was explicitly a beta — a rough take. The new plan is structured and scripted with both Nick and Ken Daniels now in the picture:
> "We're going to start over and only one bad, rough take off and landing. And now we're actually planning our route, and we're going to do this in a very structured and ordered format."

**Production philosophy:**
The eventual video content is meant to be for history's sake — serious, prepared, not improvised:
> "We have to come off as serious people, this is for history's sake."

The production pipeline Christian describes is:
1. Reteach fellowship content live in the Miro board environment (interactive class / research nights).
2. Refine into a polished session with all three.
3. Record the final version through "EB mux" for release as a proper class.

**Word and Film history:**
- Word and Film domain registered 2019 (confirmed via whois during session; initially started planning ~2013).
- Tax-exempt status approved January 19, 2017.
- First camera was donated in April 2015.
- Early live streams on YouTube / Rumble; chose Rumble to protect content from potential censorship.

**Miro board as production backbone:**
The Miro board titled "First Century Fellowship" functions as the shared research and teaching canvas. Christian shared editor access with both Ken and Nick during this session. Comments and @mentions on the board trigger email notifications, enabling asynchronous collaboration between sessions.
