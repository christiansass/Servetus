# Tithing Tool Concept: A Mutual-Fund Model for Directing Tithe Across Ministries

## Summary

During this session, Christian articulated a long-held software concept: a tithing tool that decouples the act of giving from any single ministry's claim on those funds. The core idea is that a person deposits 10% of their income into a personal tithing account and then — week to week, as God directs — allocates where those funds go across multiple ministries, individual believers, or causes.

Ken Daniels immediately compared it to a mutual fund, which Christian confirmed as the right analogy. The key differentiator from existing tools like Tithely is that the believer, not any ministry, retains ongoing directional control. The fund manager analogy is explicitly rejected: God guides the individual, not an intermediary organization.

This concept was described as a natural fit for First Century Church (the home-fellowship network Christian is building) precisely because First Century, by design, does not take donations itself. This gives it zero financial threat posture relative to existing churches and denominational ministries — including The Way International and local churches like Foundation Church. The tool would be offered as a First Century product, letting users direct tithe freely to any ministry, including ones outside the Unitarian tradition.

A secondary point: Christian noted this is also part of why Binary Ranch exists — the company to build tools that serve the mission doesn't exist yet, so he is building the company. First Century gets account 0001 as Binary Ranch's first client, followed by personal and professional accounts. The ordering reflects his stated priority: God first, family second, business third.

The conversation also surfaced the persistent pastoral concern about competing for tithe money. Christian's response to that objection is structural: a tool that returns tithe agency to the individual cannot be weaponized by any ministry as a competitive threat.
