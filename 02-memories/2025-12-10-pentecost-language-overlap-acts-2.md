# Pentecost Language Overlap — Acts 2 Analysis

**Source:** 2025-12-10-Language_overlap_in_Pentecost.md
**Date:** 2025-12-10

## Summary

Analysis of the language groups listed in Acts 2 at Pentecost, examining how many distinct languages were actually present given significant overlap, and whether the count maps neatly to 12 apostles.

## Key Finding

Acts 2 lists **peoples/regions**, not unique languages. In the 1st-century eastern Roman world, multilingual overlap was extensive — especially in Jewish diaspora communities. The number of distinct languages is fewer than the number of regions listed.

## Major Overlap Groups

### Koine Greek (massive overlap)
Groups likely Greek-speaking: Cappadocia, Pontus, Asia (Roman province), Phrygia, Pamphylia, Egypt, Libya/Cyrene, Crete, "Strangers of Rome"

### Hebrew/Aramaic
- Judea / Palestinian Jews (listed as #5) — primarily Hebrew/Aramaic

### Parthian/Eastern Languages
- Parthians, Medes, Elamites, Mesopotamia — eastern language families

## Analysis Notes

- The 12 apostles → 12 languages mapping is not linguistically supported by the text
- Overlap means fewer miracle tongues needed than regions suggest
