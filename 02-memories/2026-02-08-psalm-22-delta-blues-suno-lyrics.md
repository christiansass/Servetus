## Key Points

- **Theological frame**: Psalm 22 is the most messianic psalm; Christ quoting the opening line invokes the entire psalm per Jewish interpretive culture; His "tetelestai" (It is finished) mirrors Psalm 22:31 "He has done it" (כִּי עָשָׂה / ki asah)
- **Musical arc**: lament → mockery → suffering → trust → declaration → completion → victory
- **Structure**: verse/chorus with call-and-response feel; bridge as turning point
- **Second version** cross-references: casting lots for garments (John 19:24), gall and bitter wine (John 19:29), pierced hands and feet, "Into Your hands I commit my spirit" (Luke 23:46 / Psalm 31:5)
- **Final line**: "It is finished / It is done" — links tetelestai to Psalm 22:31

## Suno Style Prompt

> Delta blues gospel lament turning into victory hymn. Slow to mid-tempo (60–75 BPM), minor key resolving to major in final section. Sparse, raw instrumentation: slide or resonator guitar, deep upright bass, brushed drums, occasional harmonica swells. Male vocal, gravelly, restrained, reverent, prophetic — more testimony than performance. Call-and-response phrasing, dynamic arc from suffering to triumph. Influences: early Delta blues, gospel blues, spiritual lament, field holler tradition. Earthy, analog, unpolished, sacred.
