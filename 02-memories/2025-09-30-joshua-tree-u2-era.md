# U2 — Joshua Tree Era Overview

**Source:** 2025-09-30-Joshua_Tree_U2_era.md
**Date:** 2025-09-30

## Summary

Description and analysis of U2's Joshua Tree era (1987) — musical style, visual aesthetic, and cultural positioning.

## Musical Style

- **Genre**: Anthemic rock; American roots influence (blues, gospel, country)
- **Character**: Spiritual urgency; sweeping, cinematic scale
- **Sound**: The Edge's delay-drenched guitar, Bono's soaring vocals, Larry Mullen Jr.'s driving rhythms, Adam Clayton's atmospheric bass

## Visual Aesthetic

- **Photographer**: Anton Corbijn — black-and-white desert photography
- **Setting**: American Southwest; desolate landscapes evoking pilgrimage and longing
- **Feeling**: Dust, distance, prophecy

## Cultural Positioning

- U2 as "prophetic troubadours" — rock band with moral and spiritual gravity
- Communal concert experience; stadium anthems that demanded participation
- Album positioned as meditation on the American Dream vs. its failures

## Key Tracks

- "Where the Streets Have No Name"
- "I Still Haven't Found What I'm Looking For"
- "With or Without You"
- "Bullet the Blue Sky"
