# Servetus: Own Your Mind

## The Extraction

Your life is scattered across platforms you don't control.

Notes in one app. Tasks in another. Conversations in a dozen more. Each one holds a fragment of how you think, what you care about, who you are.

And they keep it. Forever.

Google knows your questions. Facebook knows your relationships. OpenAI knows your thought process — because you talk to it like a therapist, like a hairdresser, telling it your plans, your fears, your half-formed ideas.

**That's not a feature. That's an extraction operation.**

Every conversation with a cloud AI is a trade. You give context, plans, vulnerabilities, thought patterns. You get a response. If you're not extracting that value back to local storage and cleaning up after yourself, you're leaving yourself exposed.

---

## The Hostage Situation

Right now, third parties have better intelligence on you than you have on yourself. They can analyze your patterns, your vulnerabilities, your decision-making. You can't — because your data is locked in silos you can't export, can't search, can't see.

If Facebook deleted your account tomorrow, it would be like your house burning down. You'd lose years of conversations. Relationships, documented. Memories you forgot you had.

**That's not a service. That's a hostage situation.**

---

## The Exile

But here's the part people don't see until it happens to them:

When they ban you, they don't delete your data. **They take you away from it.**

Your profile still exists. Your messages, your photos, your years of history — all archived. They keep your digital shadow. They could restore it with a keystroke.

But they don't. And no one can tell you who to talk to. No employee. No support channel. No appeal. You're exiled from your own life, while they keep the records.

**It's not deletion. It's separation.**

You lose access to your history. They keep the intelligence.

I watched this happen to a friend. Not political. Not controversial. Just... gone. Twitter account down. Facebook account down. And everyone scrambling to find a human — *any* human — who could flip the switch.

No one could. Or no one would.

The data wasn't destroyed. It was confiscated.

---

## The Asymmetry

| You | They |
|-----|------|
| Lose access | Keep everything |
| No recourse | Full control |
| Can't export | Can analyze |
| Erased from the platform | Your shadow remains |

**They don't un-person you. They just lock you out of your own life and keep the keys.**

---

## The Inversion

> **Technology is only scary when others have more command of it than you do.**

What if you had a space you could trust — because you own it?

A place where your thoughts, your plans, your daily life flow into a structure you control. Where an AI assistant actually knows your context — not because a company stores your data, but because *you* store it, and you decide what to share.

Think about what forensic analysis looks like: mapping conversations, tracing patterns, identifying influence, understanding how decisions unfolded over time.

**Could you do that on your own life?**

Most people can't. But the platforms can — and do.

> **The ultimate intelligence is the intelligence you contribute to.** You should have more command of your own data than any third party.

---

## The Sovereignty Stack

**Servetus is the counterweight.**

- **Extract.** Pull your history out of every silo. Messenger exports. Google Takeout. iCloud downloads. All of it.
- **Own.** Store it locally. Plain text Markdown. No proprietary formats. No lock-in. Files you can read, edit, search, backup.
- **Analyze.** See yourself the way an analyst would — your patterns, your conversations, your trajectory. On your own terms, on your own machine.
- **Choose.** Decide what to share, with whom, when. Load relevant context into any AI model. Get value. Delete the thread. Leave no footprint.
- **Clean up.** If you're constantly clearing your sessions on public models, there's no trace left — unless they're being dishonest.

---

## The Freedom Test

> **If you're not afraid to delete your accounts, you're free.**

That's the goal. Not to abandon the platforms — but to use them without dependency. Extract value, clean up, leave no trace you didn't intend.

- Want to use ChatGPT? Load context, get value, delete the thread.
- Want to stay on Facebook? Fine — but because you *choose* to, not because you're trapped.
- Want to share everything publicly? Great. Want to share nothing? Also great.

**You decide. Because you have it.**

They can exile you from the platform. They can't exile you from your own files.

---

## Context + Rules + Any Processor

It's not just that the context persists. **The rules persist too.**

The Toolkit — your specs, your protocols, your behavioral definitions — those are *yours*. Any LLM that enters the scene inherits your rules. It doesn't bring its own defaults. It reads yours.

- How you want exports formatted
- How you want time handled
- How you want topics segmented
- What you want kept private

**The rules govern the model. Not the other way around.**

Switch from Claude to GPT? Rules still apply. Run a local Llama instance? Rules still apply. Some new model drops next year? Load it up. Rules still apply.

And when the situation changes, *you* modify the rules. Not the vendor. Not the platform. You.

---

## The LLM Is Irrelevant

Claude isn't the point. Neither is GPT, Gemini, Llama, or whatever comes next.

**If you own the context, the model becomes interchangeable.**

The value was never in the AI. The value is in *your* context — your history, your projects, your patterns, your life. The LLM is just a processing layer. A tool you plug in.

Today it's Claude. Tomorrow it might be something local, something open-source, something that doesn't exist yet. Doesn't matter. Your context persists. Your sovereignty persists.

**You're not just free from data platforms. You're free from AI platforms too.**

## Interoperability

If everyone's context is structured differently, it's just isolated vaults. Useful, but not connectable.

**Interoperability requires a standard.**

A consistent way of attaching meaning to artifacts. Metadata that travels with the file — or can be shared without the file. Relationships that can be expressed, queried, merged.

When your graph and my graph use the same schema, we can:
- Share specific nodes without exposing everything
- Merge timelines where they overlap
- Corroborate witnesses across independent sources
- Build shared context *without* a central platform

**You still decide:**
- What levels to share
- What permissions to grant
- What integrates and what stays isolated

The standard is the protocol. The permissions are yours.

## What Servetus Is

A human-readable operating system for your life.

- **One place.** All your notes, conversations, projects, and daily logs. Structured by altitude — from daily captures to 30,000-foot life maps.
- **Plain text.** Markdown files. No binary blobs. If the tools disappear, your files still work.
- **Local first.** Your context lives on your machine. You're the sole owner.
- **AI that knows you.** Any LLM reads your vault, your projects, your context. Every conversation starts where you left off — then ends when you say so.
- **A space you can trust.** Because you own it.
