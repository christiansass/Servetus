# StoryMap Sharing Model (Future)

> **Status:** Draft architecture for future implementation

## Core Concept

StoryMap is the **connective tissue** - the social layer where private vaults can selectively expose nodes for discovery and connection.

## The Privacy Pyramid

```
        ┌─────────────┐
        │  STORYMAP   │  ← Public surface (map outline)
        │  (shared)   │
        ├─────────────┤
        │   EVENTS    │  ← Selectively shareable
        ├─────────────┤
        │  WITNESSES  │  ← Private by default (ground truth)
        └─────────────┘
```

- **Witnesses** stay private (your artifacts, your evidence)
- **Events** can be shared with permission layers
- **Storymap** shows the map without exposing depth

## Serendipitous Discovery

Two users at the same baseball game:
- Both have witnesses (photos, videos)
- Both have events (the game)
- Storymap nodes overlap geographically/temporally
- System surfaces: "You were both here"
- Users choose whether to connect

**Key insight:** You can see we were at the same place without seeing each other's private notes, photos, or context.

## Permission Layers

| Level | Sees | Example |
|-------|------|---------|
| Public | Map nodes only | "Christian was in Chicago in Jan 2026" |
| Acquaintance | Events + basic metadata | "Christian attended Cubs game" |
| Friend | Events + selected witnesses | "Here's my photo from the game" |
| Trusted | Full witness chain | Artifacts, notes, context |
| Private | Everything | Your eyes only |

## Granular Sharing

- Share an event without its witnesses
- Share a witness without its artifacts
- Share an arc without all its events
- Custom groups with custom permissions
- Per-relationship trust levels

## File System Implications

Future frontmatter fields:
```yaml
sharing:
  scope: private | friends | public | custom
  groups: []           # Custom permission groups
  inherited: true      # Inherit from parent event/arc
  redactions: []       # Fields hidden at certain levels
```

## Discovery Without Exposure

The storymap surface shows:
- Geographic nodes (where)
- Temporal nodes (when)
- Category tags (what type)

It does NOT show:
- Actual artifacts
- Private notes
- Witness details
- Full context

Users opt-in to reveal more.

## Connection Flow

1. Storymap surfaces overlap: "You were both at [location] on [date]"
2. Users see anonymized match
3. Either can initiate connection request
4. If accepted, permission negotiation begins
5. Each user sets what they share with the other
6. Relationship forms with defined boundaries

## Design Principles

1. **Private by default** - Nothing shared without explicit action
2. **Granular control** - Share exactly what you want, nothing more
3. **Discoverable but protected** - Find connections without exposing details
4. **Asymmetric permissions** - I might share more with you than you share with me
5. **Revocable** - Permissions can be withdrawn

## Cross-Vault Evidence Corroboration

Discovery and social connection are one use of sharing. But the deeper purpose is
**truth certification through convergent evidence across independent vaults.**

When two people share arcs or story maps, the evidence layers should interlock based
on timecode. If your witnesses and my witnesses describe the same event — same time,
same place, same participants — and they corroborate each other, we haven't just
shared a story. We've certified a fact.

### The Problem This Solves

We are entering an era where artificially generated content is produced at a
mind-numbingly fast rate. LLMs generate slop. Deepfakes fabricate events. Screenshots
are trivially faked. A single person's record — no matter how carefully maintained —
can be questioned: "How do I know you didn't fabricate this?"

The answer is corroboration. You can fake one recording. You can fake one vault.
You cannot easily fake two independent vaults, maintained by two different people
on two different devices, whose evidence aligns on timestamps, audio fingerprints,
speaker identification, spatial positioning, and environmental conditions.

**One vault is memory. Two vaults that agree are evidence. Multiple vaults that
agree are certified history.**

### How Cross-Vault Corroboration Works

#### Arc Comparison

When User A shares an arc with User B, and User B has their own arc covering
the same time period:

```
User A's Arc: "Q1 Product Launch"
  ├── Event: Kickoff meeting, 2026-01-15
  │   └── Witness: audio recording, transcript, SVP
  ├── Event: Design review, 2026-01-22
  │   └── Witness: screen recording, notes
  └── Event: Launch day, 2026-03-01
      └── Witness: video, photos, slack logs

User B's Arc: "New Job at Company X"
  ├── Event: First team meeting, 2026-01-15      ← TIMECODE MATCH
  │   └── Witness: audio recording, transcript
  ├── Event: Design presentation, 2026-01-22     ← TIMECODE MATCH
  │   └── Witness: notes, photos
  └── Event: Product launch, 2026-03-01          ← TIMECODE MATCH
      └── Witness: video, photos
```

The system surfaces: "These arcs share 3 events with matching timecodes."

#### Evidence Interlocking

For each shared event, the corroboration engine compares evidence layers:

```
Event: "Kickoff meeting, 2026-01-15 10:00 CST"

  User A's Witnesses:
    audio: meeting_20260115.wav (SHA256: a1b2c3...)
    transcript: "Emily said we need the API done by February"
    svp: speaker_01=Christian, speaker_02=Emily
    location: Conference Room B (from calendar)

  User B's Witnesses:
    audio: team_standup_jan15.wav (SHA256: d4e5f6...)
    transcript: "Emily mentioned the API deadline is February"
    svp: speaker_01=Emily, speaker_02=Christian, speaker_03=Raj
    location: Conference Room B (from notes)

  INTERLOCKING EVIDENCE:
    ✓ Timestamps align (same 10:00 AM window)
    ✓ Location matches (Conference Room B)
    ✓ Speaker identification overlaps (both identify Emily, Christian)
    ✓ Transcript content converges ("API" + "February")
    ✓ User B has additional speaker (Raj) — not contradictory, additive
    ✓ Audio fingerprint: background noise profiles are consistent
      (same HVAC, same room acoustics)

  CORROBORATION STATUS: CERTIFIED
  Two independent vaults, two independent devices, convergent evidence.
```

#### Negative Evidence — When Vaults Don't Match

Corroboration in the negative is equally valuable:

```
Event: "Phone call with contractor, 2026-02-10 15:00 CST"

  User A claims:
    Witness: audio recording
    Transcript: "Contractor agreed to $50,000 price"
    Location: Home office

  User B (the contractor) claims:
    Witness: audio recording
    Transcript: "We discussed pricing but nothing was agreed"
    Location: Their office

  INTERLOCKING EVIDENCE:
    ✓ Timestamps align
    ✓ Both confirm a call happened
    ✗ Transcript content DIVERGES on substance
    ? Audio fingerprint comparison needed

  CORROBORATION STATUS: DISPUTED
  The event occurred (corroborated) but the substance is contested.
  Both recordings preserved. Both witnesses intact.
  Neither vault gets to overwrite the other.
```

If two people claim they were in the same room but the audio environments don't
match — different background noise, different room acoustics, no shared environmental
events — that's evidence too. The absence of corroboration is itself informative.

### The Corroboration Protocol

When vaults exchange evidence for comparison, the protocol must preserve sovereignty:

#### What Gets Shared (Evidence Metadata)

```yaml
# Corroboration exchange packet — metadata only
corroboration_offer:
  event_ref: "2026-01-15--kickoff-meeting"
  time_start: 2026-01-15T10:00:00-06:00
  time_end: 2026-01-15T11:15:00-06:00
  location_hash: "sha256(Conference Room B)"  # Hashed — proves match without revealing
  participants_hash: ["sha256(Emily)", "sha256(Christian)"]
  audio_fingerprint: "chromaprint:ABC123..."   # Acoustic fingerprint, not the audio itself
  transcript_keywords_hash: ["sha256(API)", "sha256(February)", "sha256(deadline)"]
  speaker_count: 2
  integrity:
    witness_hash: "sha256:..."
    device: "iphone-15-pro"
    continuous_recording: true
```

#### What Does NOT Get Shared (Without Explicit Permission)

- Raw audio files
- Full transcripts
- SVP prosodic data (vocal fingerprints)
- Personal notes
- Artifact contents

The comparison can happen on *hashed metadata* without either vault exposing its
raw evidence. "Do our location hashes match? Do our participant hashes match?
Do our audio fingerprints correlate?" — all answerable without revealing content.

If both parties consent to deeper comparison, they can progressively reveal:
1. First: metadata hashes (zero-knowledge corroboration)
2. Then: transcript excerpts (if hashes matched)
3. Then: audio segments (for disputed sections only)
4. Full access: complete witness chain (trusted relationship)

This follows the existing permission pyramid but applies it specifically to
evidence verification.

### Truth Certification Levels

| Level | Requirement | Meaning |
|-------|-------------|---------|
| **Uncorroborated** | Single vault, any number of artifacts | "I recorded this" |
| **Self-corroborated** | Single vault, multiple independent devices | "My devices agree" |
| **Cross-corroborated** | Two+ vaults, evidence interlocks | "Independent people agree" |
| **Certified** | Three+ vaults, evidence interlocks, integrity verified | "This happened" |
| **Disputed** | Two+ vaults, evidence conflicts on substance | "Something happened, but accounts differ" |

The jump from "uncorroborated" to "cross-corroborated" is the critical one.
It's the difference between "I said so" and "we both independently recorded it
and our records match." That's the threshold where personal memory becomes
shared evidence.

### Anti-Fabrication Properties

Why is cross-vault corroboration hard to fake?

1. **Independent capture:** Each vault's audio was recorded on a different device,
   from a different position, with different microphone characteristics. Fabricating
   consistent audio from two perspectives requires modeling room acoustics, speaker
   positions, and environmental sounds — far harder than faking a single recording.

2. **Temporal consistency:** The timecodes must align across vaults to sub-second
   precision. Audio fingerprints of shared environmental events (a door closing,
   a phone ringing) must appear at the same timestamp in all recordings.

3. **Prosodic consistency:** SVP data captures pitch, pace, energy, and rhythm.
   Two recordings of the same speaker from different positions should show
   consistent prosodic patterns with predictable acoustic differences.

4. **Environmental correlation:** Background noise, room reverb characteristics,
   and ambient sound profiles must be consistent across all recordings. You can't
   fake two recordings having the same HVAC hum at the same frequency.

5. **Progressive difficulty:** Faking corroboration across 2 vaults is hard.
   Across 3 is exponentially harder. Across N, it becomes effectively impossible
   for anything other than a state-level actor — and even then, the physics
   of spatial audio from multiple positions creates a verification surface
   that's extremely difficult to fabricate convincingly.

### Relationship to Deepfakes and AI-Generated Content

This is the antidote to the deepfake problem:

- A deepfake is a single generated artifact. Convincing in isolation.
- A Servetus-certified event is multiple independent artifacts from multiple
  independent sources that converge on the same truth.
- You can generate a fake video. You cannot easily generate a fake video
  AND a corroborating audio recording from a different angle AND a third
  recording from a third person's device AND have all the environmental
  audio fingerprints match AND have the prosodic profiles be physically
  consistent from multiple spatial positions.

The cost of fabrication scales with the number of independent corroborating
sources. Servetus makes corroboration easy for real events (just record) and
fabrication progressively impossible.
