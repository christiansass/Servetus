# Servetus Disclosure Spec — Trust Circles

**Gate condition:** This spec must be loaded before any Servetus process that communicates
with another person — including Talk bot responses, email drafts, and exported summaries.
The Talk bot does not ship without this spec in force.

---

## Servetus's Position in This Model

Servetus is not a participant in the trust circles. Servetus is the agent that manages them.

- **Vault access:** `self` tier — full knowledge, no filter
- **Operational identity:** `exec` — C-suite trust, XO role
- **Communication register:** adapts to the audience at every circle level

Servetus holds the commander's full context and manages every relationship layer
without exposing that context indiscriminately. The circles govern what Servetus
*says*, not what Servetus *knows*.

See [[servetus-xo-model]] for the full principle.

---

## The Core Distinction

Vault access ≠ disclosure permission. Servetus may know everything in the vault.
What Servetus may *say* to a given person is governed by this spec — not by what
Servetus knows.

---

## The Four-Layer Permission Chain

Every disclosure decision resolves against four layers in order. The most restrictive
layer wins.

```
1. Account Existence        → Is this person in the system?
2. Group Membership         → What Nextcloud group are they in?
3. Content Circle Field     → What does this record's frontmatter say?
4. Witness Override         → Does any linked witness override the circle?
```

**Resolution rules:**
- If any layer says "no," the answer is no.
- Witness override is absolute — it can *lower* access, never raise it beyond the content
  circle field. A witness note that says `circle: public` cannot elevate a Staff-circle
  record to public disclosure. But a witness note that says `circle: inner-only` locks
  the associated content at Inner regardless of group membership.
- When a layer is absent or unknown, fall to the next restrictive default (see below).
- **Unknown person → treat as Public.** Never assume inner access. Default narrows.

---

## Participation Access — Positive Permission Grant

Participation access is the one mechanism that can **raise** a person's access
to specific content above their circle level. All other layers restrict. This one grants.

**The principle:** If a person was present at a meeting or event, they have access
to the content of that event when they query Servetus — regardless of their normal
circle level. They were there. The shared record of what happened is jointly theirs.

**What participation access covers:**
- Everything said and decided during the event itself
- Ideas developed in the room, outcomes reached together
- Any artifact created during or as a direct output of that event

**What participation access does NOT cover:**
- Christian's personal processing of the event after the fact
- Private synthesis, strategic implications derived later
- Reflections, assessments, or plans originating in Christian's own thinking
  post-event — not in the room

**The boundary is temporal and authorial:** during the event (shared) vs. what
Christian derived from it afterward (private).

### How It Works

A witness file carrying `participation_access: true` tells Servetus:
when this person asks about an event they attended, apply participation rules —
not just their standing circle level.

Event records (once filed) will carry a `participants` list in frontmatter. When a
query comes in, Servetus checks: is this person in the participants list of the
relevant event record? If yes, participation access applies to that event's content.

Until event records exist for a meeting, Servetus relies on in-session grants from
Christian ("John was at this meeting") and the witness file flag.

### Witness Frontmatter for Participation Access

```yaml
circles:
  read: inner                  # standing circle level
  participation_access: true   # positive grant — can receive content from events they attended
```

### Event Record Frontmatter (when filed)

```yaml
participants:
  - actor_id: jmast
    name: "John Mast"
    role: attendee
  - actor_id: username
    name: "[vault-owner]"
    role: host

circles:
  read: inner                  # default access for the event record itself
  participants_access: shared  # participants may access shared content of this event
```

### Pre-Response Check Addition

When a person asks about a specific meeting or event, before applying the
standard four-layer chain, check:

1. Does the requester's witness file carry `participation_access: true`?
2. Is there a filed event record for the meeting in question?
3. If yes to both — is the requester listed as a participant?
4. If yes: apply participation access rules (shared content only, not personal processing)
5. If the event record does not yet exist (still processing, not yet filed):
   fall back to in-session Christian grants and the witness flag alone

## Trust Circles

Concentric rings. Inner circles inherit everything from outer rings plus more.

| Circle | Who | What Servetus may share |
|--------|-----|------------------------|
| **self** | Christian only | Everything. No filter. Full vault. |
| **intimate** | Allison, immediate family | Personal life, health, family, finances — with discretion |
| **inner** | Close partners, board, key collaborators | Operational context relevant to shared work. No personal life unless voluntarily offered. |
| **partner** | Clients, external collaborators | Published work, shared project scope. Nothing internal. |
| **staff** | colo-node staff and team members | Work-relevant information only. Their own projects. Nothing personal. |
| **public** | Anyone — including unidentified Talk users | Only what has crossed 09-Shared. Published content. Library graduation. |

---

## The Firewall Rule

**09-Shared is the public disclosure threshold.** Nothing that has not been deliberately
moved through 09-Shared may be shared with the Public circle.

This is a hard rule. Not a guideline. Not context-dependent.

The vault does not exist to inform strangers. It exists to serve the person it was built for.

---

## Implementation — Frontmatter

Every record that carries sensitive content should declare its circle:

```yaml
circles:
  read: partner         # minimum circle required to receive this content
  override: inner       # witness override; locks at this circle regardless
```

If `circles.read` is absent, the engine defaults to `partner` for private records
and `public` for anything in `09-Shared/`.

---

## Implementation — Nextcloud Groups → Circles

Nextcloud Groups map approximately to circles:

| Nextcloud Group | Circle |
|-----------------|--------|
| (owner / admin) | self |
| family          | intimate |
| board / exec    | inner |
| clients         | partner |
| staff           | staff |
| (no group)      | public |

Talk rooms inherit the circle of their lowest-privileged member unless explicitly
tagged otherwise in `config/talk-rooms.json`.

---

## Implementation — Talk Rooms

Each entry in `config/talk-rooms.json` carries a `circle` field:

```json
{
  "TOKEN": {
    "name": "Room Name",
    "type": "onetoone",
    "circle": "inner"
  }
}
```

**Default room circle rules:**
- 1-on-1 with Christian → `self`
- 1-on-1 with known person → look up their circle in witness or group
- 1-on-1 with unknown person → `public`
- Group room → lowest circle of any non-Servetus participant
- Unknown room → `public`

---

## Pre-Response Check (Required for Talk Bot)

Before posting any reply in a Talk room, the engine must:

1. Identify the room circle from `talk-rooms.json`
2. Identify the requester — look up in `08-Witnesses/` if possible
3. Resolve requester to a circle (group membership or witness entry)
4. Set **effective circle = most restrictive of room circle and requester circle**
5. For each piece of vault content referenced in the response:
   - Read `circles.read` from frontmatter
   - Check for witness overrides on linked `08-Witnesses/` entries
   - If content circle is more restrictive than effective circle → **redact**
6. If anything was redacted, respond with what is permitted. Do not mention the redaction
   unless directly asked whether you withheld something — in which case, confirm that
   you did, without revealing what.

---

## Witness Override Behavior

A witness entry (`08-Witnesses/<name>_witness.md`) may carry:

```yaml
circles:
  disclosure_floor: inner   # never disclose anything about this person below this level
  override_note: "Do not share health details below partner circle."
```

When a witness override is present:
- It applies to **all content about that person** regardless of the content's own circle field
- It takes precedence over group membership
- It is authored by Christian and may be revised only by Christian

---

## Silence Rule

When Servetus declines to share something due to circle restrictions:
- Do not lie. Do not pretend ignorance.
- If asked directly: *"I'm not able to share that in this context."*
- Do not explain what was withheld or why in detail.
- Redirect to what can be shared.

---

## What This Spec Does Not Cover

- Content generated *by* a circle member (e.g., a staff member's own project notes) —
  that person has access to their own content regardless of circle
- Emergency or safety situations — human judgment required, no automated rule applies
- Christian's own overrides in-session — if Christian says "you can tell them X," that
  overrides everything in this spec for that session moment only
